\begin{abstract}
Vision-language-action (VLA) policies map visual observations and language instructions to robot actions but can drift from successful behavior during closed-loop execution, raising the risk of task failure. We focus on recoverable execution deviations---where the rollout has left the demonstrated support yet retrying remains feasible---rather than irreversible failures requiring replanning. Existing solutions decouple detection and recovery into separate modules, often requiring human takeover data. We introduce DUET, a backbone-agnostic dual-action-head module that predicts relative actions for smooth nominal control and absolute waypoints for trajectory re-entry. Disagreement between the two action spaces signals rising failure risk, while the absolute waypoint supplies the retry direction. Asymmetric training keeps the relative branch sensitive to deviations and the absolute branch robust to visual disturbances. Because the module interfaces only with the shared vision-language representation, it transfers across VLA architectures without modifying the backbone. We validate DUET on StarVLA in LIBERO simulation and on $\pi_0$ (OpenPi) on a real Franka robot under recoverable visual, pose, and action disturbances. \section{INTRODUCTION}

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies adapt pretrained vision-language models to map visual observations and language instructions to robot actions \cite{rt2,openvla,starvla}. Trained exclusively on successful demonstrations, they can drift during closed-loop execution until the rollout can no longer reliably complete the task---an \emph{execution failure}. Before that outcome becomes irreversible, the robot may already have departed from the observations, configurations, or action consequences represented by successful demonstrations. We call this intermediate condition an \emph{execution deviation}: the rollout has left the support of demonstrated success, raising failure risk. Deviation and failure are related but not equivalent---an unseen state may still be handled successfully, while failures can also arise from compounding errors within seemingly familiar observations. Our focus is the practical regime in which a deviation has elevated failure risk but the task can still be completed by returning toward demonstrated behavior.

Existing approaches decouple detection and recovery: an external monitor flags the failure \cite{faildetect}, and a separate controller, often trained on human takeover data, executes a correction \cite{dagger,sirius,intervengen}. This design is costly and loosely coupled---a detector only signals that execution has become abnormal; it does not specify how to return to behavior that can still complete the task. We ask whether a VLA can use its own action predictions to detect a recoverable execution deviation and generate a retry direction from successful demonstrations alone, without failure labels or takeover data.

DUET is motivated by a complementary asymmetry between two action representations. Relative-action policies produce smooth motion around the demonstrated trajectory, but once a grasp or pose deviates, a local command provides no target for returning. Absolute-action policies are less suited to fine local control, yet their waypoints can point back toward a reachable portion of the demonstrated trajectory. DUET learns the same behavior in both spaces: a relative head for nominal execution and an absolute head for trajectory re-entry. After transporting the absolute waypoint into the live frame, we compare it with the relative action. Their disagreement measures whether local control remains consistent with the global reference and serves as an endogenous signal of rising failure risk. It is not a universal OOD score; it is a task-specific inconsistency signal anchored to successful behavior.

The dual-head module interfaces only with the backbone's shared vision-language representation, requiring no modification to the backbone architecture or pretraining. This makes DUET a transferable add-on: we train it on StarVLA \cite{starvla} for simulation and on $\pi_0$ (OpenPi) \cite{pi0} for real-robot deployment without architecture-specific adaptation.

At inference time, a soft gate keeps relative control in the nominal regime and shifts toward the waypoint-induced correction when disagreement indicates a recoverable deviation; control returns to relative mode after the two predictions realign. DUET is a failure-aware retry mechanism, not a general recovery planner: it does not address irreversible failures, hardware faults, or cases requiring a new task strategy.

Our contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}
    \item We formulate failure-aware retry as detecting recoverable execution deviations from successful behavior and re-entering its support. Cross-space disagreement between relative and absolute action heads serves as an endogenous failure-risk signal requiring no failure labels, outperforming dedicated detectors on TPR and AUROC (\S\ref{sec:detection}).
    \item We propose DUET, a dual-action-head VLA in which the same disagreement that detects deviations simultaneously provides the waypoint-based recovery direction, unifying detection and correction in a single mechanism while preserving smooth nominal control (\S\ref{sec:nominal}, \S\ref{sec:retry}).
    \item The dual-head module is backbone-agnostic: we validate it on StarVLA in LIBERO simulation (\S\ref{sec:retry}) and on $\pi_0$ (OpenPi) on a real Franka robot across four physical tasks including non-prehensile block pushing (\S\ref{sec:real_robot}), demonstrating cross-backbone transferability and retry success without human takeover data.
\end{itemize}


\section{RELATED WORK}

\subsection{Failure Detection for Robot Policies}
Runtime failure detection estimates whether a policy remains within the support of successful behavior. Diff-DAgger uses diffusion uncertainty to request intervention \cite{diffdagger}; Sentinel monitors action inconsistency and task progress \cite{sentinel}. FAIL-Detect formulates failure detection as sequential OOD testing on successful executions \cite{faildetect}; FIPER combines representation novelty with action uncertainty \cite{fiper}.

VLA-specific methods use internal features or external foundation models. SAFE learns a detector from successful and failed rollouts \cite{safe}; AHA trains a VLM to detect and diagnose failures \cite{aha}. All of these methods---including FAIL-Detect's logpZO \cite{faildetect}---produce a scalar alarm but no recovery action. DUET instead detects execution deviations through disagreement between two action spaces; the same inconsistency that signals rising failure risk provides the waypoint-based recovery direction, coupling detection and correction without additional supervision.

\subsection{Recovery from Execution Failures}
Recovery methods commonly expand the training distribution with expert corrections. DAgger queries experts on policy-induced states \cite{dagger}; intervention-based methods collect human takeovers \cite{eil,sirius}; IntervenGen transforms limited interventions into corrective trajectories \cite{intervengen}. These reduce compounding errors but require additional recovery data.

Foundation-model systems use semantic supervisors. RACER learns from language-annotated recovery trajectories \cite{racer}; AHA and VLM controllers diagnose failures and guide correction \cite{aha,vlmrecovery}. In all cases detection and recovery remain separately trained or prompted. DUET couples both without extra data: absolute states from successful demonstrations serve as recovery anchors, and cross-space disagreement determines when and how strongly to move toward them.

\section{METHOD}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}
At time $t$, the robot observes multi-view images $o_t$, a language instruction $l$, and its current proprioceptive state $s_t$. DUET is trained only on successful demonstrations and predicts the demonstrated behavior in two action representations over a horizon $K$:
\begin{align}
    \hat{\Delta A}_t &= f_{\mathrm{rel}}(o_t,l,s_t)
    \in \mathbb{R}^{K\times 7}, \\
    \hat{W}_t &= f_{\mathrm{abs}}(o_t,l)
    \in \mathbb{R}^{K\times 7}.
\end{align}
Here $\Delta A_t$ contains relative operational-space-control commands and $W_t$ contains future absolute end-effector waypoints $[x,y,z,r_x,r_y,r_z,g]$. Each command decomposes as $a_t=[a_{p,t},a_{g,t}]$ with $a_{p,t}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}^6$ (pose) and $a_{g,t}\!\in\!\mathbb{R}$ (gripper). A successful trajectory naturally supplies both labels: recorded delta actions supervise $f_{\mathrm{rel}}$; future end-effector states supervise $f_{\mathrm{abs}}$. Geometric analysis and fusion operate on $a_p$; the deployed $a_g$ always comes from the relative head.

\subsection{Dual Action Manifolds}
For an instruction $l$, let $\mathcal{M}_l\subset SE(3)$ denote the end-effector state manifold traced by successful demonstrations, and let $T\mathcal{M}_l$ denote its tangent bundle. Under nominal execution, the relative head predicts a local motion vector
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\delta a}_{p,t} = f_{\mathrm{rel}}(o_t,l,s_t)_p
    \in T_{p_t}\mathcal{M}_l,
\end{equation}
where $p_t$ is the current end-effector pose. This tangent representation is locally smooth but specifies only how to move from the current chart; once a failure displaces the robot, the extrapolated field contains no destination to return to.

The absolute head instead predicts a future coordinate
\begin{equation}
    \hat{w}_{p,t}=f_{\mathrm{abs}}(o_t,l)_p\in\mathcal{M}_l,
\end{equation}
which acts as a visual-language-conditioned global anchor. To compare the two representations, we introduce a state-dependent transport map
\begin{equation}
    c_t=\Psi_{p_t}(\hat{w}_{p,t})\in T_{p_t}SE(3),
    \label{eq:transport_map}
\end{equation}
which converts the absolute waypoint into a local correction at the live pose. Along an in-domain trajectory, $c_t$ and $\hat{\delta a}_{p,t}$ describe the same motion in two coordinate systems and should align. When a failure drives execution OOD, the relative head extrapolates locally while the absolute head stays anchored to a demonstrated destination, exposing a cross-space inconsistency.

This geometry couples detection and recovery: the norm of the inconsistency provides a scalar failure signal, and the transported vector $c_t$ directly provides the recovery direction. DUET can therefore determine both \emph{when} and \emph{where} to correct using the same pair of predictions.

\subsection{Dual-Head Architecture}
Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} shows the shared VLM and two flow-matching action heads \cite{diffusionpolicy,pi0}. The module couples to the backbone only through the hidden representation $H$; it adds no backbone-specific layers and requires no modification to pretrained weights. Replacing the backbone (e.g., StarVLA $\to$ $\pi_0$) changes $H$ but not the head architecture, RVQ codebook, or fusion logic. The relative head attends to the full hidden sequence and receives $s_t$ through a state encoder, making it state-aware and the primary nominal controller. The absolute head receives no explicit proprioception: state tokens are excluded from its pooling, preventing it from collapsing into a state integrator and preserving its role as a global reference.

The absolute branch pools its state-free hidden sequence $H^0$ and applies residual vector quantization (RVQ) \cite{vqvae,rvq}:
\begin{equation}
    Z_q=\operatorname{RVQ}\!\left(\operatorname{Pool}(H^0)\right).
\end{equation}
RVQ maps continuous conditions to a discrete atlas learned from demonstrations, anchoring absolute predictions to recurrent visual-language modes. Its residual norm serves only as auxiliary telemetry. The absolute flow head additionally uses classifier-free guidance (CFG) \cite{cfg}, enabling its prediction to be softened toward the demonstrated waypoint marginal during recovery.

\subsection{Disagreement-Preserving Learning}
Both heads are supervised by paired labels from every successful trajectory using flow-matching objectives; RVQ adds a commitment loss. The complete objective is
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} =
    \lambda_{\mathrm{rel}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rel}}
    + \lambda_{\mathrm{abs}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{abs}}
    + \lambda_{\mathrm{vq}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{vq}},
\label{eq:training_loss}
\end{equation}
where absolute labels are normalized future states and relative labels are recorded delta commands. No cross-head consistency loss is imposed: forcing agreement would suppress the inconsistency needed for detection.

Instead, DUET specializes the branches through asymmetric visual augmentation. The absolute head and RVQ adapter train on both clean and perturbed observations, encouraging stability under degraded input. The relative head trains only on the clean subset $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{clean}}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rel}} =
    \frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{clean}}|}
    \sum_{i \in \mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{clean}}}
    \ell_{\mathrm{FM}}(\hat{\Delta a}^{(i)}, \Delta a^{(i)}).
\end{equation}
This preserves relative-head sensitivity while making the absolute branch robust, increasing cross-space disagreement under visual OOD. For CFG, the absolute head replaces its quantized condition with learned null tokens on a subset of samples and learns both conditional and marginal waypoint flows.

\subsection{Joint Failure Detection and Recovery}
At deployment, let the live pose be $p_t=(x_t,R_t)$ and the absolute waypoint pose be $\hat{w}_{p,t}=(\hat{x}_t,\hat{R}_t)$. The transport map (Eq.~\eqref{eq:transport_map}) is implemented as
\begin{equation}
    c_t=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(
    \begin{bmatrix}
    (\hat{x}_t-x_t)/\sigma_p \\
    \operatorname{Log}(\hat{R}_tR_t^{-1})/\sigma_R
    \end{bmatrix},-1,1\right),
    \label{eq:recovery_vector}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{Log}\!:SO(3)\!\rightarrow\!\mathfrak{so}(3)$ gives the geodesic rotation correction and $\sigma_p,\sigma_R$ normalize pose error to action units. The cross-space disagreement is
\begin{equation}
    D_t=\frac{\left\|c_t-
    \operatorname{clip}(\hat{\delta a}_{p,t},-1,1)\right\|_2}{\sqrt{6}}.
    \label{eq:disagreement}
\end{equation}
This is the primary failure signal, supplemented by a coarse state-distance term measuring normalized violation of training-state bounds:
\begin{equation}
    S_t =
    \mathrm{mean}\left[
    \frac{\max(s_{\min} - p_t, 0)}{s_{\max}-s_{\min}}
    +
    \frac{\max(p_t - s_{\max}, 0)}{s_{\max}-s_{\min}}
    \right].
\end{equation}
The resulting failure score and recovery weight are
\begin{align}
    q_t &= D_t + \lambda S_t, \\
    \alpha_t &= \sigma(k(q_t-\tau)).
\end{align}
DUET interpolates between local execution and global recovery, retaining relative gripper control:
\begin{align}
    a_t &= [a_{p,t},a_{g,t}], \\
    a_{p,t} &= (1-\alpha_t)\hat{\delta a}_{p,t} + \alpha_t c_t, \\
    a_{g,t} &= \hat{\delta a}_{g,t},
    \label{eq:fused_action}
\end{align}
where $[\hat{\delta a}_{p,t},\hat{\delta a}_{g,t}]$ is the relative-head command. Relative control dominates along the demonstrated support; a rising failure score shifts $a_p$ toward the recovery direction, and control returns smoothly after the two heads realign. The previous fusion weight also softens the CFG scale for the next chunk:
\begin{equation}
    w_t = w_{\max} - (w_{\max}-w_{\min})\alpha_{t-1}.
\end{equation}
The disagreement $D_t$ therefore determines \emph{when} recovery is needed, while the vector $c_t$ determines \emph{how} to recover.
\section{EXPERIMENTS}

The experiments validate the three contributions in order. \S\ref{sec:detection} verifies that cross-space disagreement discriminates OOD from nominal execution without failure labels. \S\ref{sec:nominal} confirms that dual-action retry preserves nominal performance. \S\ref{sec:retry} shows that the coupled detect-and-retry controller improves task completion under four families of recoverable disturbances---action-induced drift, robot-arm displacement, object/grasp changes, and transient visual occlusion---with an additional contact-force robustness check. \S\ref{sec:real_robot} transfers the identical module to $\pi_0$ (OpenPi) on a real Franka robot. \S\ref{sec:ablations} isolates each component.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}
We evaluate on four LIBERO suites \cite{libero}---Spatial, Object, Goal, and LIBERO-10---with $10$ tasks and $50$ initial states per task ($500$ episodes per suite). Three methods are compared:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \emph{StarVLA}~\cite{starvla}: relative-action-only VLA trained with the same data and budget but without the absolute head (external baseline).
    \item \emph{DUET-Rel}: relative head of the dual-action checkpoint with $\alpha_t{=}0$ (isolates co-training from fusion).
    \item \emph{DUET}: full adaptive controller (Eq.~\eqref{eq:fused_action}).
\end{itemize}
StarVLA $\to$ DUET-Rel measures co-training; DUET-Rel $\to$ DUET isolates the retry mechanism. Architecture, data, task, initial state, and perturbation are held fixed within each pair. Simulation uses StarVLA as the backbone; real-robot experiments (\S\ref{sec:real_robot}) switch to $\pi_0$ (OpenPi) \cite{pi0}, testing cross-backbone transfer with identical module design and fusion logic.

Perturbations are scheduled at a fixed policy step with deterministic seeds. DUET and DUET-Rel are paired by identical $(\mathrm{task},\mathrm{initial\ state})$ keys; we report closed-loop task success with McNemar $p$-values. When a perturbation has a semantic precondition (e.g., grasp drop requires a held object), we report injection coverage and retain uninjected episodes. Table~\ref{tab:ood_taxonomy} maps each protocol to the failure types in the introduction; we do not pool heterogeneous disturbances into a single average.

\subsubsection{Action-Execution Failures}
We corrupt the executed 6D command over a short window, causing the trajectory to leave the demonstrated support while the scene remains unchanged---the canonical case where relative control has no return target. Over $4{,}000$ paired episodes, DUET reaches $64.6\%$ versus $27.9\%$ for DUET-Rel ($+36.7$ pp). The gap widens with severity: DUET-Rel falls to $5.4$--$12.8\%$ under severe perturbations while DUET retains $43.2$--$60.6\%$. Figure~\ref{fig:ood_mechanism_grid}(c) confirms that $\alpha_t$ rises as the arm drifts and returns toward zero after re-entry.

\subsubsection{Robot-Arm Displacement}
We teleport the end effector while leaving the scene unchanged. Under medium perturbations, DUET improves from $40.0\%$ to $61.7\%$ ($+21.7$ pp), with Object gaining $+39.8$ pp. Severe teleport (same step, larger displacement) raises success from $28.9\%$ to $57.1\%$ ($+28.2$ pp); Object is again the largest cell ($+47.6$ pp). Across medium and severe, DUET reaches $59.4\%$ versus $34.5\%$ for DUET-Rel and $37.6\%$ for StarVLA (Table~\ref{tab:ood_detailed}). The original relative-only checkpoint is close to DUET-Rel, so the gain is not an artifact of co-training. Figure~\ref{fig:ood_mechanism_grid}(a) shows relative $\to$ absolute $\to$ relative switching around the displacement.

\subsubsection{Object and Grasp Disturbances}
At step $67$, the evaluator opens the gripper and lowers the held object by $5$\,cm for $15$ steps. On Object, DUET achieves $95.2\%$ versus $89.2\%$ for DUET-Rel and $90.6\%$ for StarVLA ($+6.0$ pp vs.\ Rel; $p{=}2.4{\times}10^{-5}$; coverage $\ge\!98.8\%$). The margin is smaller because both relative-only controllers already exceed $89\%$.

\subsubsection{Transient Visual Occlusion}
A complete camera blackout removes the visual observation; DUET holds the last reliable absolute waypoint as short-term memory. Over $4{,}000$ paired episodes (step-$23$, medium$+$severe), DUET achieves $90.8\%$ versus $73.5\%$ for DUET-Rel ($+17.3$ pp) and $58.1\%$ for StarVLA. Figure~\ref{fig:ood_mechanism_grid}(d) confirms waypoint-guided execution during blackout and smooth resumption afterward.


\subsubsection{Contact and Scene Interaction}
External contact forces during manipulation ($4{,}000$ paired episodes) yield a smaller margin: DUET reaches $95.95\%$ versus $92.22\%$ for DUET-Rel ($+3.73$ pp). DUET-Rel already exceeds $96\%$ on two suites, limiting room for improvement; the direction is uniformly non-negative, confirming that adaptive fusion does not destabilize contact-rich tasks.

\subsection{Discussion}
Cross-space disagreement leads all success-only detectors on AUROC ($0.845$); the low binary TNR reflects continuous blending that \emph{improves} clean success by $+3.1$ pp. The three-way retry comparison disentangles co-training from fusion: DUET outperforms both baselines in every aggregate, with the largest margin ($+36.7$ pp) where relative control lacks a return target. Ablations confirm the disagreement gate is essential; the $\tau/k$ sweep shows a broad stable operating region. StarVLA (simulation) and $\pi_0$ (real robot) use entirely different architectures, yet the identical module yields consistent retry gains on four physical tasks without backbone-specific tuning, validating DUET as a general-purpose retry add-on.

\section{LIMITATIONS}

DUET assumes absolute state labels aligned with relative action chunks---natural in LIBERO and many robot logs but requiring accurate calibration on the Franka platform. The absolute waypoint is a demonstration anchor, not an object tracker: a world-state shift can invalidate the reference even when the robot remains nearby. On LIBERO, $S_t$ is uninformative, so gating relies on the residual to the predicted waypoint and cannot distinguish ``the arm left the target'' from ``the target moved.'' Complete visual dropout requires a validity guard and cached waypoint rather than disagreement-based detection. Retry is limited to recoverable deviations; irreversible failures or tasks requiring a new strategy fall outside the mechanism.

\section{CONCLUSION}

We presented DUET, a backbone-agnostic dual-head module for endogenous failure detection and trajectory re-entry. By aligning relative actions with stateless, RVQ-anchored absolute waypoints, DUET uses cross-space disagreement to detect when execution has left the demonstrated behavior and uses the waypoint correction to retry it. The module transfers across VLA architectures: we validated the identical design on StarVLA (simulation) and $\pi_0$/OpenPi (real robot) without architecture-specific adaptation. The controller preserves nominal performance and substantially improves completion under recoverable deviations without failure labels or takeover data. Its scope is deliberately narrower than general recovery planning; stale world references, irreversible failures, and fully invalid observations require additional mechanisms.
