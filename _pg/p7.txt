F. Real-Robot Evaluation
We next test transfer beyond the simulation backbone.
Because DUET attaches only to the shared vision-language
representation, the same dual heads, fusion rule, and validity
guard move from StarVLA to π0 (OpenPi) [26] without
changing the DUET architecture.
a) Platform and protocol.: We deploy on a 7-DoF
Franka Emika Panda with a parallel-jaw gripper, a wrist-
mounted Intel RealSense D405, and a third-person Orbbec
FemtoBot camera, executing delta joint-position commands
at 20 Hz. The backbone is π0-base (PaLIGemma 2B encoder,
Gemma 300M action expert, flow matching) [26]; each task
is fine-tuned for 5k steps from the public checkpoint on 50
VR-teleoperated demonstrations (Fig. 2). Four tasks cover
complementary primitives (Fig. 8): pick-and-place, block
pushing, drawer opening, and whiteboard wiping.
Each task is evaluated under nominal execution and two
OOD families (N=20 trials per cell): a random-direction
EEF shift (robot state) and an object displacement (world
state). Intervention phases and magnitudes are task-specific.
Random directions are paired across π0 and π0+DUET to
control trial difficulty.

90.0% nominal success of π0. Under robot-state EEF shifts,

G. Discussion
The experiments both support and bound the mechanism.
Disagreement ranks failures and drives recovery when the
live pose leaves Ml; it is weaker for world-state changes
and uninformative under invalid images, which the validity
guard handles. For arm-state OOD, the shared-checkpoint
comparison attributes the gain to adaptive gating rather than
co-training, a pattern that persists from StarVLA in simu-
lation to π0 on hardware. Visual dropout separates the two
effects: asymmetric training lifts DUET-Rel over StarVLA,
while the guard adds robustness as the outage lengthens. The
broad τ–k plateau and successful backbone transfer indicate
that recovery is not tied to per-suite gate tuning.
